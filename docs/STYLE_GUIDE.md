# Writing style guide

How every quiz script, reveal and pinned comment should sound. Built from Giulia's four General Knowledge scripts, three published captions and *The Ten Telltale Signs of AI-Generated Text* (Michael G Wagner). The writing task and the style lint both use this file.

## Giulia's voice

**Questions**
- One plain sentence with no padding. Example: "What is the capital city of Kazakhstan?"
- Specific and surprising beats broad and obvious. A myth-busting angle is welcome.

**Reveals**
- The answer, punchy and short, then **one** emoji, then a quick quip, myth-bust or fun fact.
  - "They're called a troop! 🐒 Not a pack, not a herd. A TROOP!"
  - "It stands for 1,000! 🏛️ Roman maths, people!"
  - "Austria! 🥐 Most people say France but it was invented in Vienna."
- Witty, funny remarks, metaphors and puns, kept short and sharp.
- Rotate the openers so they don't all start the same way: "It's…", "That's…", "The answer is…", or just the answer on its own.

**Emphasis and tone**
- Capitals for emphasis (ALL-NEW, FINAL, MUCH) and exclamation marks.
- Warm sign-off ("Byeiiii", "thanks for playing!").

**Recurring beats**
- **Intro:** says which quiz this is, then the format line: no multiple-choice options, you'll need to rely purely on memory. Then "Drop your score in the comments at the end" and "Let's get started!"
- **Half-time (after question 25):** a check-in, e.g. "That's our first 25 done! Keeping track of your score? 📝 Things are about to get harder."
- **Outro:**
  - "That's a wrap!" and a score request in the comments.
  - Thumbs up and subscribe, "we're here for you on Thursdays and Sundays".
  - Earlier quizzes are linked in the description.
  - The end-screen video or playlist named out loud.

**Spelling:** Australian (colour, metres, maths, fibre, characterised).

## AI tells to remove

The style lint fails a quiz if it finds any of these in a script, reveal or pinned comment:

1. **Dashes:** no em dashes (—), en dashes used as dashes, or spaced hyphens ( - ). Use a full stop or a comma instead.
2. **Groups of three for rhythm:** no "X, Y and Z" lists used as filler. Two items, or four or more, are fine when they're factual.
3. **Hedging and stock transitions:** "it's important to note", "generally speaking", "to some extent", "from a broader perspective".
4. **Inflated vocabulary:** delve, underscore, harness, illuminate, facilitate, bolster, tapestry, realm, beacon, intricate, palpable, cacophony.
5. **Formulaic structures:** "From X to Y" ranges, and clauses tacked onto the end of a sentence such as ", revealing…" or ", making…".
6. **Monotony:** vary sentence length. No run of reveals with the same structure.

## Accuracy

- Every answer carries **at least two independent sources**, recorded in the quiz file.
- Where sources disagree, the question is rewritten or dropped.
- A myth-bust ("Not Egypt, Sudan!") needs sources that state the myth-busting fact itself.
- Known error to avoid repeating: Quiz #2 said babies have "around two hundred and seven" bones. The correct figure is about 270–300.

## Exception: captions

The YouTube description and caption template is **kept exactly as Giulia wrote it** (agreed 28 Oct), including its existing dashes and lists. The only change is the schedule line, which must read Thursday 3PM & Sunday 1PM ET.
